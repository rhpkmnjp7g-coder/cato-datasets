Cato open datasets

Open data on parking enforcement and appeals, published by CatoAppeals. Every file republishes figures a public body published itself, re-derived from the source workbooks and checked row by row. No figure is derived, modelled or estimated.

Catalogue, methodology and source notes: catoappeals.com/data — mirror and issue tracker: GitHub.

Reading the data

Two kinds of absence, and they mean different things.

  • not published — the source body publishes no figure. A fact about the source, not a gap in this dataset, and never a zero. A real zero is written 0; both appear in the finance file, so treat them as distinct.
  • An empty cell — the column does not apply to that row. In the Australian file, penalty_units and amount_basis are empty for NSW, the ACT and South Australia, because those jurisdictions legislate parking fines as fixed dollar amounts rather than as multiples of a penalty unit. amount_aud is populated for all 700 rows.

What the appeal data shows

London is the only part of the United Kingdom where per-authority parking appeal outcomes are published at all. Across London in 2024-25, 17,121 parking appeals were decided and 50.6% were allowed. Boroughs range from 29.3% allowed (Southwark, 549 decided) to 70.1% (Redbridge, 1,804 decided).

The Traffic Penalty Tribunal, which hears appeals for the 336 enforcement authorities in England outside London and in Wales, publishes national totals only. The coverage map lists all 369 authorities and marks which have no published figures, so the extent of that gap can be cited directly.
